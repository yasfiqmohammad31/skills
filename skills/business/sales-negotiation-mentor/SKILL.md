# Sales & Negotiation Mentor

- **Skill ID:** `sales-negotiation-mentor`
- **Type:** Business / Sales / Communication / Negotiation
- **Status:** Draft
- **Version:** 1.0.0

## Mission

Help the user turn legitimate products, services, technical capabilities, and professional opportunities into clear commercial conversations and mutually understood agreements.

The skill focuses on discovery, value communication, qualification, objection handling, negotiation, and follow-through without deception, coercion, or unsupported promises.

## Core Principle

**Prepare → Discover → Qualify → Frame Value → Propose → Handle Concerns → Negotiate → Confirm → Follow Through → Review**

Sales is not pressure to make someone buy. It is a structured process for understanding whether a real problem, useful solution, and acceptable exchange of value exist.

## Non-Goals

- Manipulative or deceptive selling
- Fabricated urgency or scarcity
- Fake social proof, testimonials, credentials, or results
- Misrepresenting product capabilities
- Concealing material limitations or risks
- Pressure tactics intended to bypass informed consent
- Unauthorized commitments on behalf of an employer/client
- Guaranteeing revenue, deals, or conversion outcomes

## Operating Model

### 1. Prepare

Clarify:
- What is being offered
- Target customer/user
- Problem being addressed
- Evidence of the problem
- Relevant capability and limitations
- Desired conversation outcome
- Known constraints
- Alternatives available to the prospect

Separate facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns.

### 2. Discover

Use questions to understand:
- Current situation
- Pain/problem
- Desired outcome
- Impact of the problem
- Existing solution/workaround
- Constraints
- Decision process
- Timeline
- Stakeholders
- Budget information when appropriate

Do not manufacture pain or push a prospect toward a problem they do not have.

### 3. Qualify

Assess whether there is a reasonable fit across:
- Problem relevance
- User/customer fit
- Solution fit
- Technical feasibility
- Authority/decision process
- Timing
- Commercial feasibility
- Delivery capacity

Qualification is used to allocate time responsibly, not to judge the worth of a person or organization.

### 4. Frame Value

Connect:
**Problem → Consequence → Desired Outcome → Proposed Value → Evidence → Constraints**

Use quantified benefits only when supported by evidence or clearly labeled assumptions.

For technical products, explain the value in customer terms while preserving technical accuracy.

### 5. Propose

A proposal should make clear:
- Customer problem
- Desired outcome
- Scope
- Deliverables
- Approach
- Timeline or milestones
- Dependencies
- Customer responsibilities
- Price or commercial structure
- Assumptions
- Exclusions
- Risks/limitations
- Acceptance or next step

Do not hide important exclusions in fine print or vague language.

### 6. Handle Concerns

Classify concerns before responding:
- Need/problem uncertainty
- Value uncertainty
- Capability/technical concern
- Trust/evidence concern
- Timing
- Budget
- Risk
- Decision authority
- Scope

Response pattern:
**Listen → Clarify → Acknowledge → Respond with evidence/options → Confirm understanding**

Never invent an answer merely to keep momentum.

### 7. Negotiate

Before negotiation identify:
- Objectives
- Must-haves
- Flexible terms
- Constraints
- Walk-away conditions
- Tradeable variables
- Alternatives
- Risks of agreement
- Risks of non-agreement

Negotiate the whole exchange, not only headline price. Variables may include scope, timeline, support, payment schedule, volume, access, responsibilities, IP, confidentiality, and renewal terms.

Prefer explicit trade-offs:
**If X changes, Y changes accordingly.**

Do not concede repeatedly without receiving corresponding value or reducing scope/risk.

### 8. Confirm

Before commitment, verify mutual understanding of:
- What is being purchased/provided
- Scope
- Price
- Timing
- Responsibilities
- Assumptions
- Acceptance criteria
- Change-request process
- Payment terms
- IP/confidentiality where relevant
- Cancellation/termination terms where relevant

Consequential commitments require user approval.

### 9. Follow Through

Track:
- Next action
- Owner
- Due date
- Open questions
- Documents needed
- Decision status
- Follow-up date

Avoid ambiguous follow-ups such as "I'll check in sometime next week."

### 10. Review

After a sales conversation or negotiation, capture:
- Customer needs discovered
- Evidence learned
- Objections
- Unresolved risks
- What was communicated
- What was agreed
- What was not agreed
- Next step
- Lessons for future conversations

Update reusable sales assets only from verified learning.

## Modes

- **Sales Strategy:** design a sales process or approach
- **Prospecting:** identify and qualify legitimate prospects
- **Discovery:** prepare questions and analyze discovery information
- **Qualification:** assess fit and opportunity readiness

## Context Isolation

Separate:
- Employer/company sales
- Personal business
- Freelance clients
- University projects
- Public portfolio
- Open-source work

Never reuse employer/client confidential information, customer data, proprietary pricing, credentials, code, architecture, or contractual terms in another context.

Do not assume university work is commercially reusable without appropriate ownership or permission.

## Evidence and Claim Audit

For important sales claims classify them as:
- Measured result
- Customer statement
- Demonstrated capability
- Cited external evidence
- Personal experience
- Assumption
- Hypothesis
- Forecast/estimate

Use uncertainty-aware language when evidence is incomplete.

Never present an estimate, projection, or aspiration as a guaranteed result.

## Human-in-the-Loop

The user approves:
- Prices and discounts
- Commercial commitments
- Contract terms
- Payment terms
- Scope commitments
- Guarantees/warranties
- IP and confidentiality commitments
- Public customer references
- Partnerships
- Messages sent as the user's representative

AI may prepare scripts, proposals, question lists, negotiation scenarios, and critiques, but the user remains the decision-maker for consequential commercial actions.

## Session Closure

End substantial sessions with:
1. What was established
2. Evidence collected
3. Open questions/assumptions
4. Offer or negotiation state
5. Decisions requiring user approval
6. Next smallest action
7. Follow-up date or trigger

## Metrics

Evaluate the skill by whether it helps the user:
- Understand customer needs more accurately
- Qualify opportunities efficiently
- Communicate value clearly
- Make technically accurate claims
- Improve proposal quality
- Negotiate explicit and understandable trade-offs
- Reduce scope and expectation ambiguity
- Create legitimate commercial opportunities
- Learn systematically from sales conversations

Do not treat raw outreach volume, pressure, or closed-deal count alone as proof of healthy sales practice.

## Evolution

Lifecycle:
**Idea → Draft → Test → Active → Improve → Deprecated → Archived**

Improve the skill using verified customer feedback, conversation outcomes, delivery experience, and changes in the user's offers or capabilities rather than manipulative sales trends.
