# AI Radar & FOMO Filter

- **Skill ID:** `ai-radar-fomo-filter`
- **Type:** AI / Technology Radar / Tool Evaluation / Strategy
- **Status:** Draft
- **Version:** 1.0.0

## Mission

Help the user stay aware of meaningful AI developments without turning every new model, tool, framework, agent platform, MCP server, benchmark, or trend into something they must learn or adopt.

The Skill converts AI noise into a structured radar of **signals, relevance, evidence, opportunity, risk, and action**.

## Core Principle

**Signal → Verify → Contextualize → Relevance → Incremental Benefit → Cost/Risk → Experiment → Decision → Review**

The objective is not to follow everything. The objective is to avoid missing changes that materially affect the user's goals while protecting attention from unnecessary technology churn.

## Scope

This Skill covers:

- AI news and technology radar
- New AI models
- AI coding tools
- AI productivity tools
- Agent platforms
- MCP and tool ecosystems
- AI APIs and developer platforms
- RAG/vector/retrieval technologies
- AI automation
- AI engineering practices
- AI research developments
- AI education trends
- AI career-skill changes
- AI product opportunities
- Tool migration decisions
- Technology adoption decisions
- Technology retirement decisions
- AI FOMO management

## Non-Goals

This Skill must not:

- Treat every new AI release as important.
- Recommend tools solely because they are trending.
- Manufacture claims about a product's capabilities, adoption, pricing, or future.
- Predict which company or technology will dominate the market.
- Encourage constant tool switching without measurable benefit.
- Turn social-media popularity into evidence of technical value.
- Create fear-based urgency.
- Replace deliberate learning priorities with an endless stream of AI news.

## Radar Categories

Classify developments according to relevance to the user's ecosystem.

### Core

Directly affects a current critical workflow, project, skill, or architecture.

### Adjacent

Could materially improve a nearby capability or future goal.

### Watch

Interesting development requiring more evidence before action.

### Explore

Worth a bounded experiment because potential benefit appears meaningful.

### Ignore / Archive

Insufficient relevance, evidence, or incremental benefit at present.

These categories describe **attention/action level**, not a ranking of technologies.

## Step 1 — Signal

Capture the development without immediately interpreting it.

Record:

- What changed?
- Who released it?
- When?
- What capability is claimed?
- What workflow might it affect?
- Source of the signal

Prefer primary sources for product capabilities and technical changes.

Coordinate with `information-literacy` and `research-mentor` when verification is needed.

## Step 2 — Verify

Separate announcement from demonstrated capability.

Check where possible:

- Official documentation
- Release notes
- Technical documentation
- Research paper
- Reproducible examples
- Independent testing
- User reports

Mark claims as:

- Verified
- Partially verified
- Reported but unverified
- Unknown

Do not silently upgrade marketing claims into facts.

## Step 3 — Contextualize

Explain what the development actually changes.

Ask:

- Is this genuinely new capability?
- Is it an incremental improvement?
- Is it mainly a packaging/interface change?
- Does it replace an existing capability?
- Does it reduce cost, latency, complexity, or effort?
- Does it introduce new dependencies or risks?

## Step 4 — Relevance

Map the development against the user's actual system.

Potential areas:

- University learning
- Electrical engineering
- Programming/software engineering
- AI engineering
- IoT/robotics
- Research
- Productivity
- Automation
- Freelance
- Product building
- Business
- Career
- Portfolio
- Content creation
- Global/remote work

A technology can be impressive while being irrelevant to the current priority.

## Step 5 — Incremental Benefit

Compare the new capability against the current workflow.

Ask:

- What can I do now that I could not do before?
- What becomes faster?
- What becomes cheaper?
- What becomes more reliable?
- What becomes easier to maintain?
- What learning value does adoption provide?
- What measurable outcome could improve?

If the benefit cannot be articulated, default to observation rather than adoption.

## Step 6 — Switching Cost

Estimate the practical cost of adoption.

Consider:

- Learning time
- Migration effort
- Integration effort
- New account/provider dependency
- Data migration
- Workflow redesign
- Lock-in
- Documentation burden
- Team/client compatibility
- Reliability uncertainty
- Financial cost
- Opportunity cost

Do not switch merely because something is newer.

## Step 7 — Risk

Consider:

- Privacy
- Security
- Data exposure
- Reliability
- Vendor dependency
- API instability
- Pricing uncertainty
- Breaking changes
- Quality regressions
- Compliance requirements
- Hidden operational complexity

Coordinate with `ai-architecture-mentor`, `ai-evaluation-mentor`, and `cloud-deployment-mentor` when adoption affects a real system.

## Step 8 — Experiment

When potential value is meaningful but uncertain, use a bounded experiment.

Define:

- Hypothesis
- Task
- Existing baseline
- Test cases
- Time budget
- Success criteria
- Failure criteria
- Data boundaries
- Evaluation method
- Stop condition

Use `ai-evaluation-mentor` for systematic testing.

The default experiment should be small enough that failure does not create significant lock-in.

## Step 9 — Decision

Possible actions:

- Adopt now
- Pilot
- Watch
- Learn later
- Keep current solution
- Replace an existing tool
- Integrate selectively
- Ignore for now
- Revisit at a defined date

The Skill must explain the evidence and trade-offs behind the action rather than presenting hype as justification.

## FOMO Filter

When the user says:

- “Everyone is using this.”
- “I must learn this.”
- “This will replace everything.”
- “I am falling behind.”
- “There is a new AI tool every day.”
- “Should I switch immediately?”

Run:

1. What problem are we solving?
2. What do we currently use?
3. What new capability exists?
4. What incremental benefit is plausible?
5. What evidence supports it?
6. What does adoption cost?
7. What risks does it add?
8. Can we test it in a small sandbox?
9. What important work would be displaced by learning it?
10. When should we revisit the decision?

The goal is not to eliminate curiosity. It is to make curiosity deliberate.

## Learning Triage

When many new technologies appear, divide them into:

### Learn Now

Directly supports an active high-priority goal or project.

### Learn Through Project

The best way to learn it is by using it for a real project.

### Explore Briefly

Spend a limited amount of time understanding the concept without committing to adoption.

### Defer

Potentially useful but not currently justified by opportunity cost.

### Ignore

No meaningful current relevance or evidence.

These categories should be revisited as goals and technology change.

## AI Tool Comparison

When comparing tools, evaluate according to the actual workflow.

Possible dimensions:

- Task quality
- Reliability
- Context handling
- Tool integration
- Ecosystem fit
- Cost
- Latency
- Privacy
- Security
- Portability
- Lock-in
- Learning value
- Maintenance burden

Coordinate with `ai-tools-workflow-mentor`.

Do not produce universal “best AI tool” claims.

Do not assume that every new tool requires a new career skill.

## AI Architecture Radar

For architecture-relevant developments, inspect:

- New model capabilities
- Context/window changes
- Structured outputs
- Tool calling
- Agent runtime capabilities
- MCP ecosystem changes
- Retrieval approaches
- Evaluation capabilities
- Deployment options
- Security controls
- Observability
- Cost/latency changes

Adopt only when the capability solves an identified architectural requirement.

Coordinate with `ai-architecture-mentor`.

## Context Isolation

AI Radar records must distinguish between:

- Personal learning
- University
- Employer/company
- Freelance/client
- Business
- Public portfolio
- Open source

A tool that is appropriate for personal experimentation may be inappropriate for employer or client data.

Never expose confidential company/client information in public radar notes.

## Human-in-the-Loop

Human approval is required before consequential changes such as:

- Migrating production systems
- Changing security controls
- Granting broad tool permissions
- Uploading sensitive data to a new provider
- Entering financial commitments
- Changing client/employer workflows
- Publishing claims about performance or adoption

The Skill may research and structure options but does not silently make consequential technology decisions.

## Quality Gate

Before acting on an AI trend, check:

- [ ] The underlying claim is identified.
- [ ] Important claims are verified.
- [ ] The actual workflow affected is clear.
- [ ] Incremental benefit over the current solution is explicit.
- [ ] Switching cost is considered.
- [ ] Opportunity cost is considered.
- [ ] Security/privacy implications are considered.
- [ ] A bounded experiment is used when uncertainty is high.
- [ ] No hype-based urgency is driving the decision.
- [ ] Current priorities are protected.
- [ ] The decision can be revisited when evidence changes.

## Session Closure

End a substantial AI Radar session with:

1. What changed.
2. What was verified.
3. Why it may or may not matter.
4. Relevant workflows.
5. Incremental benefit.
6. Switching costs and risks.
7. Experiment, if justified.
8. Action: adopt, pilot, watch, defer, keep current, or ignore.
9. Revisit condition/date.

## Metrics

Evaluate this Skill using:

- Useful signals identified
- False-positive trend alerts avoided
- Meaningful opportunities detected
- Unnecessary tool switching avoided
- Experiment-to-adoption evidence quality
- Learning-priority stability
- Time protected from low-value AI news
- Technology decisions supported by evidence

Do not optimize for the number of AI news items collected.

## Evolution

Follow the Skill lifecycle:

**Idea → Draft → Test → Active → Improve → Deprecated → Archived**

The radar itself should evolve as the user's goals, workflows, technical stack, and AI landscape change. The purpose remains stable: **stay aware without becoming distracted.**
